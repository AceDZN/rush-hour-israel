# Curriculum mapping and accuracy review

Rush Hour Israel — שעת שיא uses the exact Workbook snapshots in `docs/curriculum/` as scope authority. The missions, schematic maps, budgets, capacities and compressed clock are original teaching scenarios. These scenarios do not reproduce current Israeli routes, exact GIS, operating timetables or current population statistics. Workbook is not being represented as Ministry approval, and no learning gains have been measured.

## Source discrepancy and assessment boundaries

The exact transport snapshot conflicts internally: its concept cards classify an antenna with fixed facilities and vehicles/devices with moving equipment, while the “קשרים במרחב” explanation describes antennas and cellular networks as moving with the user. This project does not score, quiz or require the ambiguous terms “תשתית נייחת/ניידת”. Runtime explanations should use concrete “מתקנים/מסילות/כבישים” and “כלי רכב” where needed.

The sources also use broad teaching generalisations about core/periphery, road expansion, city form and regional conditions. The game assesses only the shown authored scenario. It does not teach that all peripheral residents lack services, that all cities form concentric rings, that all widened roads necessarily fail, or that relief alone determines settlement. No current legal, policy, fare, demographic, geologic-rate or infrastructure statistic is needed for scoring.

The terrain's coast is west, highlands inland and rift east when represented. Names denote regional contexts on a schematic teaching atlas. The game does not simulate geological formation in a rush-hour wave. Infrastructure cost/grade units are simplified model parameters, not engineering advice. Icons, labels, land-use shapes and patterns must carry meaning in addition to colours.
