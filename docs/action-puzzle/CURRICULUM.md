# Grab, rotate, rescue — curriculum mapping

This document describes the tactile action-puzzle campaign in `src/action/campaign.ts`, not the historical line-drawing game or the earlier proposed progression. Players move and rotate physical road pieces. Buses retain their identities and passengers through bends and return trips. All required individuals must arrive; there is no introductory failure clock or majority threshold. Stars reward move economy, not geography vocabulary answers.

The supplied Workbook snapshots define the scope. The maps, road pieces, seats, heights, timing and scenario counts are original teaching constructions. They are not exact Israeli geography, real operating routes, engineering standards or measured learning outcomes. A successful reference solution establishes model solvability; it does not establish novice understanding, fun or curriculum mastery.

## Rules that make geography consequential

Reciprocal ports alone are insufficient. Water rejects ordinary road and requires the bridge's authored support axis. Rock and quiet land reject road placement. A ramp connects only when the heights of its endpoints match neighbours. Bus-only and rail-only corridors restrict a complete trip; actual transfer changes the vehicle while preserving the person. A local service plot changes whether the local group can finish, and an unserved edge group blocks an all-person win.

The runtime uses shortest-edge-count routing over the player's valid connections. Tee pieces are connectivity junctions, not manually controlled switches. When a complete path is absent, a bus may advance along a locked approach and then brake before the editable puzzle. The game does not simulate discretionary turns, an exploratory wrong-facility visit or live traffic assignment. These limits should not be replaced by stronger learning claims in instructional copy.

The six-seat bus and fourteen-seat train are teaching capacities. Passengers board once per genuine mode leg and remain on the same vehicle at ordinary corners. A finite bridge and same-mode following clearance can create a queue; the simulation does not model all traffic conflicts at intersections. Holding a road removes real connectivity. Occupied/currently reserved pieces are locked, and undo restores a complete earlier board-and-simulation snapshot with its time and passenger state.

## Source discrepancies and nonclaims

The transport snapshot conflicts internally on fixed/mobile infrastructure. Its concept descriptions place antennas with facilities and vehicles/devices with moving equipment, while another explanation describes antennas/cellular networks as moving with users. **Fixed/mobile terminology remains unassessed.** Concrete roads, bridges, stations, vehicles and trips are sufficient for the game.

Source modules use broad generalisations about urban form, centralisation and transport expansion. These boards do not assert that every city forms concentric rings, every peripheral resident lacks services, every wider road induces the same number of trips, or terrain alone determines development. Quiet land is an authored protection rule, not a decibel or legal standard. The game does not teach geological formation from ramp geometry. No Ministry approval, current demographic/statistical accuracy or measured learning gain is claimed.

## Verification boundary

The independent V3 suite is `tests/action/*.test.ts`, run with:

```sh
node --import tsx --test tests/action/*.test.ts
```

At this mapping's initial check, all **52** tests passed: twenty independent rule fixtures, twenty-four authored campaign witnesses and eight counterexamples/alternate-plan checks. These cover persistent identity and seats; real bends/return trips; occupied locks; held swaps/cancel/undo; bridge support/occupancy; ramp heights; mode transfer and final walking; local services; all-group completion; induced demand; delayed closure recovery; and alternate plans. No browser state or victory is injected by these tests.

V1 and V2 test counts do not carry forward. A witness is model evidence only. Normal-control browser observation, comparison with the new tactile mockups, first-minute discoverability, touch comfort and an unprompted human's enjoyment require separate evidence. Human fun and student learning remain unvalidated by this suite.
