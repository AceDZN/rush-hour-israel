# Rebuilt campaign curriculum mapping

This mapping describes the new continuous-line, persistent-vehicle game. V1's action sequence, deadlines and 85% win rule are historical and are not the rebuilt assessment. Each mission now requires all authored individuals to reach a suitable service, plus any explicit nuisance/land constraint. Soft time and efficient length award optional stars; they do not impose an introductory failure clock.

Maps, route shapes, budgets, vehicle seats, timing, noise and footprint are original schematic teaching values. They are not current Israeli infrastructure, exact GIS, engineering specifications or measured learning outcomes. The six supplied Workbook snapshots define scope; no Ministry approval is implied.

## Accuracy boundaries

The transport snapshot conflicts internally on fixed/mobile infrastructure: its concept cards put antennas with facilities and vehicles/devices with moving equipment, while another explanation describes antennas/cellular networks as moving with users. This terminology remains **unassessed**. Use concrete facilities, routes, stops and vehicles instead.

The source pages contain broad teaching generalisations about city structure, centralisation and road expansion. The rebuilt game assesses only its shown scenario. It does not claim that every city has concentric rings, every peripheral resident lacks service, terrain alone determines development, or every new road induces the same response. No present legal, fare, demographic, engineering or real operating-route claim is necessary.

## Verification boundary

Independent rebuilt-model tests are in `tests/rebuild/`; V1 test counts do not carry forward. The reference solutions verify all-person solvability and relevant rule constraints. First-minute discoverability, error recovery, vehicle continuity in the rendered world and appeal require separate actual-control observation. Student learning remains unevaluated.
